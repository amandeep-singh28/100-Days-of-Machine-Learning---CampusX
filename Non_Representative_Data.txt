(III) NON-REPRESENTATIVE DATA
================================================================================

DEFINITION:
Non-representative data means your training data does not properly represent 
the real-world population, yet you still trained the model on it.

PROBLEM:
When your model is trained on non-representative data, it may look good 
(good fit), but in reality it will make wrong predictions because the data 
doesn't reflect reality.


SIMPLE EXAMPLE - Linear Regression:
================================================================================
- You train a Linear Regression model on non-representative data
- Result: The model gives a good line of fit
- Reality: The line is WRONG because the data was biased
- Prediction: The model will fail on real-world data


REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE - T20 World Cup Survey:
================================================================================
Question: "Who will win the T20 World Cup?"

Two different problems can occur:


1. SAMPLING BIAS (Wrong Way of Collecting Data)
================================================================================

WHAT IS IT?
Your data collection method is flawed from the start.

EXAMPLE:
- You conduct survey ONLY IN INDIA ❌
- Result: Everyone says "India will win!"
- Reason: Indians naturally support India
- Problem: You didn't ask people from other countries

HOW TO FIX:
Conduct survey GLOBALLY ✅
- Ask people from all countries
- Get a balanced perspective
- Now data is representative!

KEY POINT:
This is SYSTEMATIC BIAS - your method is wrong, not your luck.


2. SAMPLING NOISE (Bad Luck / Random Variation)
================================================================================

WHAT IS IT?
You collect data the RIGHT WAY, but bad luck creates random fluctuations.

EXAMPLE:
- You conduct survey in different countries randomly ✅ (correct method)
- True answer: 50% think India will win
- But by bad luck, you randomly picked more India supporters
- Your survey shows: 60% think India will win (wrong estimate!)
- Reason: Pure randomness, not because of bad collection method

HOW TO FIX:
Ask MORE PEOPLE ✅
- Larger sample size = less effect of random luck
- More data = more stable results

KEY POINT:
This is RANDOM ERROR - not your method's fault, just unlucky sampling.

  → RANDOM VARIATION (no consistent bias)


REAL MODEL EXAMPLE:
================================================================================

SCENARIO 1 - Biased Data (Sampling Bias):
❌ Train model ONLY on India data
❌ Model learns: "India always wins!"
❌ Real-world test: Model fails completely

FIX:
✅ Train on data from ALL countries
✅ Model learns balanced predictions
✅ Real-world test: Model works well!


SCENARIO 2 - Lucky/Unlucky Sample (Sampling Noise):
⚠️ Train on global data (correct method)
⚠️ But randomly picked more India supporters
⚠️ Model is slightly biased (but could be fixed)

FIX:
✅ Use LARGER sample from global population
✅ Random noise cancels out with more data
✅ Model becomes more accurate


KEY TAKEAWAYS:
================================================================================

1. Non-representative data = Your training data doesn't match real world

2. Two types of non-representative data:
   
   A) SAMPLING BIAS
      - Problem: Wrong way of collecting data
      - Example: Survey only in India
      - Fix: Change your collection method
   
   B) SAMPLING NOISE
      - Problem: Bad luck in random selection
      - Example: Randomly picked too many India supporters
      - Fix: Collect more data

3. Priority: Fix BIAS first, then NOISE

4. A model trained on non-representative data:
   - May look good on training data
   - Will perform poorly on real-world data
