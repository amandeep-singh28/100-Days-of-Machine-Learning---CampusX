-> Stemming vs Lemmatization
Firstly understand these two words:-
(i)Heuristic (Rules which are defined by humans)
It is like a shortcut rule but does not gurantees to always be true.
For example:-
If a person is carrying an umbrella, its probably ranining.
This is not always true


(ii)Lingustics
Linguistic means "related to language."

It comes from the word Linguistics, which is the study of language.

For example:
What is a noun?
What is a verb?
What is the root form of a word?
How are sentences formed?

These are linguistic concepts.
So:
Linguistic = related to the knowledge and structure of a language.


Now:-
(i)Stemming(It is the process of reducing a word to its root form by removing prefixes or suffixes using heuristic rules without considering the actual meaning of the word)
Idea
Cut off prefixes/suffixes using simple rules.

Example:
playing → play
played  → play
plays   → play
Sounds good, right?

Now look at:
studies → studi
Oops 😅
The result is not even a proper English word.

Another example:
running → run
Good.

history → histori
Not good.

(ii)Lemmatization
Lemmatization is smarter.
Instead of blindly cutting letters, it uses vocabulary and linguistic knowledge.

Example:
playing → play
played  → play
better  → good
running → run
studies → study

Notice:
studies → study
which is a valid English word.

Which is better: Stemming or Lemmatization?
Lemmatization generally produces more meaningful words and is more accurate than stemming. However, stemming is computationally faster. The choice depends on the application and performance requirements.

****Module 3 - Tokenization****
Tokenization is the process of splitting text into smaller units called tokens, such as words or sentences, which can then be processed by NLP algorithms.

Why Tokenization is Needed??
Suppose we have:
I love NLP

A computer cannot directly work on the entire sentence.
First it breaks it into:
["I", "love", "NLP"]

Then later we convert these tokens into numbers.
This is why tokenization comes before vectorization.

Types of Tokenization
1. Word Tokenization
Break sentence into words.
Example:
I love cricket
↓
["I", "love", "cricket"]

2. Sentence Tokenization
Break a paragraph into sentences.
Paragraph:
I love cricket. NLP is interesting. AI is powerful.
↓
[
"I love cricket.",
"NLP is interesting.",
"AI is powerful."
]


****Module 4 - Bag of Words (BoW)****
This is the first Vectorization technique
    Machine learning models need numbers, not words

-> Working of BoW
Suppose we have two documents:-
Doc1:- I love pizza
Doc2:- I love burger

After preprocessing and tokenization
Doc1:- ["i", "love", "pizza"]
Doc2:- ["i", "love", "burger"]

Step1:- Build vocabulary
Collect all unique words
["i", "love", "pizza", "burger"]
Vocabulary size = 4

Step2:- Count Word Frequencies (from vocabulary)
Doc1: [1, 1, 1, 0]
Doc2: [1, 1, 0, 1]

Bag of Words is a text vectorization technique that converts text into numerical vectors by counting the frequency of words in a predefined vocabulary.

Limitations of BoW?
- Ignores word order
I love pizza
pizza love I

For both the vocabulary and word frequency will be same

- Ignores context/meaning
good
excellent
Different words, but similar meaning.
BoW treats them as completely unrelated.

- Vocabulary can become huge
If you have 50,000 unique words:
Vector size = 50,000
which becomes inefficient.

****N-Grams****
Observation for BoW:-
(i)No word order
I love pizza
pizza love I
Same vector
(ii)No meaning
love
adore
Different vectors despite similar meaning

An N-gram is a sequence of N consecutive words

- Unigram (N = 1)
Sentence:- I love NLP
Output:- ["I", "love", "NLP"]

- Bigram (N = 2)
Sentence:- I love NLP
Output:- ["I love", "love NLP"]

- Trigram (N=3)
Sentence:- I love NLP
Output:- ["I love NLP"]

- Why N-Grams Matter?
Look at this sentence
not good
Using BoW: ["not", "good"]
The model may focus upon good and can think that it is a positive sentence

But if we will use "Bigram":
["not good"]
Now the negative meaning is preserved
This is one of the reasons N-gram became popular

Example:-
Sentence: I do not like pizza
(i)Unigrams:-["I", "do", "not", "like", "pizza"]
(ii)Bigrams:-["I do", "do not", "not like", "like pizza"]
(iii)Trigrams:-["I do not", "do not like", "not like pizza"]

Take every group of N consecutive words


****TF-IDF****
Problem with BoW and N-Grams:
    They were giving equal importance to each and every word.
For example:-
Let's say we have two documents
Doc1: the movie is good
Doc2: the movie is bad

Vocabulary:- ["the", "movie", "is", "good", "bad"]

BoW will count:
Word	Doc1
the	    1
movie	1
is	    1
good	1
bad	    0

But, the most useful word for prediction is good or bad, Right??
Words like the, is, am, are are getting the same weightage/importance.

But TF-IDF says:
    Words that occur frequently in a document but rarely across the entire corpus should reveive higher importance

- Method
(i)For calculating the weightage of every word, we need to calculate two things of a word:- TF(Term Frequency) & IDF (Inverse Document Frequency) = TF * IDF

(ii)
Formula for TF:
TF(t,d) => (Number of occurence of term t in document d) / (Total number of terms in the document d)

Formula for IDF:
IDF(t) => log base e(Total number of documents in the corpus) / (Number of documents with term t in them) + 1
Why +1?
Because log base e (1) is 0, Word weightage would be completely ignored. That's why added 1

Example:-
D1 - people watch campusx
D2 - campusx watch campusx
D3 - people write comment
D4 - campusx write comment

TF(people, D1) -> 1 / 3 {Term frequency of people in D1}
TF(campusx, D2) -> 2 / 3

IDF(people) -> loge (4 / 2)
IDF(campusx) -> loge (4 / 3)
IDF(watch) -> loge (4 / 2)
IDF(write) -> loge (4 / 2)
IDF(comment) -> loge (4 / 2)

Vocabulary:
    people      watch       campusx     write       comment
D1  1/3 * (0.69 + 1)                                        
D2
D3
D4

IDF -> How rare a word is across all documents.

****Word Embeddings(Word2Vec)****
BoW were having a limitation:
    good, excellent
Both are different words, different vectors, No relationship.
But as humans, we know that both terms are different
So Word2Vec was invented to solve this Problem

BoW-> BoW is just creating vectors and 1 means present, 0 means absent, so it cannot understand the meaning.

For example:-
We have these sentences
I love cricket
I love football
I enjoy cricket
I enjoy football

love, enjoy => appear in similar context
cricket, football => appear in similar context

So Word2Vec says:-
    If two words appear in similar contexts, they probably have similar meanings.

BoW vs Word2Vec
BoW
good -> [1, 0, 0]
excellent -> [0, 1, 0]
bad -> [0, 0, 1]

But Word2Vec
good      -> [0.82, 0.41, 0.73]
excellent -> [0.80, 0.43, 0.71]
bad       -> [-0.75, 0.12, -0.64]

good, excellent have similar vectors. But bad is completely different

- Important Difference
BoW & TF-IDF create vector for documents
While Word2Vec create vector for words

For example:-
text = "I love NLP"
Word2Vec will create vector for each word
I      -> [0.1, 0.2, 0.3]
love   -> [0.8, 0.7, 0.6]
NLP    -> [0.4, 0.5, 0.9]

Now, we need one vector for the sentence
So, New_vector = Average of all vectors
(I + Love + NLP) / 3
[
 (0.1+0.8+0.4)/3,
 (0.2+0.7+0.5)/3,
 (0.3+0.6+0.9)/3
]

New_vector => [0.43, 0.47, 0.60]

Note:-If the words are appearing in the same context, than their vectors would be closer to each other

- Proper Pipeline for Word2Vec
Step1: Get vectors for each word
Step2: Combine them (Average of all word vectors to create sentence vector)
Step3: Feed the senetence vector into Models like Logistic Regression, SVM, etc

Making of sentence vectors known as "Sentence Embeddings"

Why??
Because in many task we need a complete vector for a sentence. For example:-
In Chatbots, message => I want to study NLP, We cannot rely on Word vector because Chatbots would not be able to understand, we need to give a full vector.

Also there is one more major limitation of Word2Vec:-
(i)Sentence level information is lost because when we create the sentence vector by doing the average of all the word vector. Now we cannot tell which words were present, which words were similar, the relationship between the words {A Lot of information is lost}


-> Sentence Embedding?
A dense numerical vector that represents the meaning of an entire sentence.

Word2Vec generates embeddings for individual words, whereas sentence embeddings generate a single vector representing the meaning of an entire sentence.

- In text classification, the categories are already known(Supervised Learning).
For example:-

The model learns from labeled examples.

- There are 10,000 articles but nobody has labelled them, so the machine has to discover topics on its own (Unsupervised Learning)

Example articles:
    Virat Kohli scored a century.
    India won the cricket match.
    IPL season starts next month.

The model may discover:
    Topic 1 = Sports

****LDA (Latent Dirichlet Allocation)****
Suppose we have millions of unlabeled articles, and it is not possible to read each article manually.
LDA helps us discover hidden topics from the collection of documents.
It analyzes the words present in the documents and identifies groups of words that frequently occur together.

For example, LDA may generate:
Topic 1:
cricket, virat, match, team, IPL
Topic 2:
python, AI, NLP, machine learning

By looking at these words, we can infer that:
Topic 1 -> Sports
Topic 2 -> Technology

Thus, instead of reading every document, we can understand the major themes present in the corpus.
